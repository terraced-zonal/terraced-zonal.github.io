A Linear-Time Construction on 𝕊2 Achieving a Sublinear Energy Gap on Smale’s Seventh Problem

TerracedZonal and four prior constructions at the same N. Each point’s Voronoi cell is colored by its share of the energy or, in the other views, by the shape of its cell. Each algorithm card notes the algorithm’s exact energy gap1 per point for the given N and the algorithm’s asymptotic complexity and accuracy2.

  1. Energy gap. E = ∑i<j log(1/‖xi − xj‖), each pair counted once. Cards show the gap per point (E − EBHS)/N above the Brauchart–Hardin–Saff asymptotic energy EBHS; lower is better. Below it: E itself, summed exactly over all pairs. Paper: §1 and App. A.1.
  2. Complexity and accuracy. Complexity: time to generate the N points. Accuracy: how the excess E − EBHS over the asymptotic energy (note 1) grows with N. TerracedZonal: Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 4.3 (under locality assumption (A)). Published constructions: complexity from their defining formulas; linear excess as measured in the paper’s Table 5 (App. F).
  3. Energy per point. Each point’s share of the excess: δi = ½∑j≠i log(1/‖xi − xj‖) − EBHS/N (half of each pair’s energy goes to each of its two points), summed exactly over all pairs. Its mean over the points is the card’s gap per point (note 1); points below the asymptotic per-point energy take the left color of the bar, points above it the right. Each card’s histogram uses the color bar’s scale, the same for all cards (its end bins collect values beyond ±0.05; the triangle marks the mean). Colors saturate at ±0.05; values for single points include contributions from distant points (e.g. ripples in the density) that largely cancel in the total.
  4. Perimeter excess P/Phex − 1, where Phex is the perimeter of the regular spherical hexagon of the same area: 0 for a perfect cell, at least 2.4% for any pentagon. A geometric diagnostic, not an energy bound; cards show its mean and the share of cells above 1%. Paper: Fig. 1 and App. B.1.
  5. Cell anisotropy √(λmax/λmin) − 1 of the cell’s second-moment tensor: how elongated the cell is (near 0 for regular pentagons and heptagons). Paper: App. B.1.
  6. Pentagons / heptagons. A cell’s number of sides is its number of neighbors. By Euler’s formula, ∑(6 − sides) = 12 on any sphere; 4 or 8+ sides get darker shades.
  7. Admissible N. The Fibonacci lattice is defined only for odd N, and EqAreaIco only for N = 10T + 2 with T = m2 + mn + n2; at other N their cards show N/A. ZonalEQ, Hammersley, TerracedZonal and TZ-R take every N.
  8. TZ-R is a slow optimization-based method that uses TerracedZonal as an initialization (Riemannian L-BFGS with defect moves), shown only as a reference (hidden unless the checkbox above the cards is ticked) and not ranked against the constructions. Paper: §5 and App. F.
